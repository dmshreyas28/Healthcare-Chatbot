Common Indian Medications:

1. Dolo 650 (Paracetamol)
- Used for fever and mild to moderate pain
- Common brand name in India for paracetamol/acetaminophen
- Available over the counter
- Dosage should be prescribed by a doctor

2. Crocin (Paracetamol)
- Another popular paracetamol brand in India
- Used for headache, fever, body ache
- Should not exceed recommended dosage

3. Combiflam (Ibuprofen + Paracetamol)
- Combination pain reliever
- Used for severe pain, fever
- Should be taken with food
- Requires doctor consultation

4. Disprin (Aspirin)
- Used for pain relief and fever
- Also used for heart conditions (low dose)
- Should be avoided in children with viral infections

5. Zincovit (Multivitamin)
- Common multivitamin supplement in India
- Contains zinc, vitamins, and minerals
- Used for immunity and nutritional support

Indian Healthcare System:

- AIIMS: All India Institute of Medical Sciences - Premier medical institutions
- PHC: Primary Health Centers - First point of contact for rural healthcare
- CHC: Community Health Centers - Secondary level healthcare
- Ayushman Bharat: Government health insurance scheme covering 50 crore Indians
- CGHS: Central Government Health Scheme for government employees
- ESI: Employee State Insurance for organized sector workers

Common Health Issues in India:

- Dengue: Mosquito-borne viral infection, common during monsoon
- Malaria: Parasitic infection transmitted by mosquitos
- Typhoid: Bacterial infection from contaminated food/water
- Diabetes: High prevalence in India (Type 2 diabetes)
- Hypertension: Growing concern in urban and rural areas
- Tuberculosis (TB): Still prevalent, government provides free treatment

Traditional Medicine:

- Ayurveda: Traditional Indian system of medicine
- AYUSH: Ministry overseeing Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy
- Common Ayurvedic herbs: Ashwagandha, Triphala, Tulsi, Turmeric
- Note: Traditional medicines should be used alongside, not replacing modern medicine

Drug Regulations in India:

- CDSCO: Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation
- Schedule H: Prescription-only medications
- Schedule X: Controlled substances requiring special prescription
- Always consult registered medical practitioners for prescription drugs

Emergency Services:

- Call 108 for ambulance services in most Indian states
- Call 102 for pregnant women and infant emergency transport
- Government hospitals provide emergency care
- District hospitals available in all districts
